Where are driverless-car crashes most likely?

Expected autonomous vehicle (AV) collisions for every census tract in San Francisco and Los Angeles. The model is trained on Waymo's 2025–26 collision filings to the CPUC and checked against an independent record: the DMV's 2019–24 crash reports from every company. Scores are relative (a 1–100 percentile with a 90% range). They rank places; they do not rate whether AVs are safe.

Coverage layer · observed

Tracts in driverless deployment filings

Tracts listed each month in CPUC filings (Waymo and Cruise). Until the counts were redacted in 2025, the filings also showed how many of those tracts had any trip.

Listed in filing Listed and had ≥1 trip

“Listed” is an upper bound

A tract counts as covered when an operator lists it in its CPUC monthly filing. Before March 2024, 95–98% of the ~250 listed San Francisco tracts had trips. When the footprint expanded to 1,498 tracts, only 13–40% had any trip (Mar–Dec 2024): newly authorised areas are listed long before they are driven. Blue means authorised service area. It does not mean tested.

No record is not the same as dangerous

A Flag means the public has no driverless safety evidence from places like this one: it is a statement about evidence. San Francisco's transportation authority has proposed opening each new geography to an operator only after it shows a performance record (SFCTA permitting framework, 2025); Flags mark where no public record of that kind exists yet. Place risk comes from a Waymo collision model using census and OpenStreetMap road features (2025–26; held-out deviance −39 to −44% on unseen neighbourhoods; city ranking matches NHTSA crash reports, ρ 0.74; and, out of time, it ranks where 2019–24 DMV-reported AV crashes happened, ρ 0.39 within county, including a different operator's). Read it as a ranking with a 90% range, and rank within a county: across metros the level can be off by about ×/÷2.6. It is not a count forecast. The model counts all reported collisions, whoever was at fault, so it measures exposure to conflict and assigns no blame.

Disengagement reporting has ended

California replaced disengagement reports with new metrics from 1 July 2026, so 2020–2024 is a closed record. The risk layer describes the era of supervised testing and should not be read as a current safety rating.
